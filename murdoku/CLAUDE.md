## Where the rules came from (and what was NOT copied)

The format was researched from public descriptions (murdoku.com, murdoku.fans FAQ,
allthings.how's explainer) and the official site's public puzzle catalogue (70 puzzles: very easy
5x5 through expert 16x16, one person per row/column, suspects count = grid size). **No official
puzzle was copied**: the puzzle files are not publicly served, and they are someone's creative
work anyway. Every case here is generated on the device.

The rules this game uses, stated once (they are also in `js/engine.js`'s header):

- N x N floor plan, split into rooms by walls. N people, one of them the victim (Victor, a skull).
- Exactly one person in every row and every column.
- Blocking objects (plant, bookshelf, piano, TV) cannot be stood on; a chair or a rug can.
- "Beside" = orthogonally touching AND in the same room (a wall breaks it). This matches the
  official definition as published on murdoku.fans.
- Every clue is true, and the clues alone decide the board.
- The murderer is the one person alone in a room with the victim. That is the REVEAL; the solver
  never uses it as a rule. The generator only guarantees the solution has exactly that shape.

## Engine (`js/engine.js`, pure, headless)

- `generate(tier, seed)` is deterministic. Steps: grow rooms from random seeds (every room >= 3
  cells); a random permutation places people (victim last); reject unless the victim's room holds
  exactly one other person; scatter furniture; then build the clue set.
- **Two solvers.** `countSolutions` (search, MRV + forward checking) proves uniqueness.
  `humanSolve(level)` solves only by deductions a person makes: level 1 = singles (one square
  left; a row/column only one person can reach) and the knock-on of each placement; level 2 adds
  "a person stuck on one line owns it" and clue-chain reasoning (arc consistency, incl. "alone"
  against every other person); level 3 adds two people sharing the same two lines.
- **Every tier is generated by the human solver**, so no case ever needs a guess: clues are added
  (kind picked by the tier's weights first, then an instance, so "not in X" with one instance per
  room cannot drown the rest) until `humanSolve` finishes it, then minimised (a clue is dropped if
  the case stays solvable; everyone keeps at least one clue). Levels: easy 1, medium 2, hard 3,
  expert 3. **Floors**: medium, hard and expert are rejected if level 1 alone solves them.
  An expert floor of 2 was tried and rejected: almost no 8x8 case survived it.
- Sizes: easy 5x5 (4 rooms), medium 6x6 (4-5), hard 7x7 (5-6), expert 8x8 (6-7).
- "Same room as" clues never involve the victim: they would hand over the answer without solving
  the board.
- Measured (node, desktop, 60 seeds per tier): easy ~1 ms, medium ~4 ms, hard ~8 ms median
  (max ~30), expert ~30 ms median (max ~120 ms). A phone is a few times slower; the UI paints a
  "building" state before it generates.

## Persistence and stats

- Settings: `gamehub.murdoku.v1` (`{tier}`), saved on selection.
- Progress: `gamehub.murdoku.save.v1` (`{v, puzzle, pos, marks, elapsedMs, solved, recorded}`),
  the whole puzzle stored as-is.
- Stats: `recordResult('murdoku', tier, true)` once per solved case (the `recorded` flag guards
  it). **No sub-counter** (total/byDiff only), so the three-edit sub-counter rule does not apply;
  `js/game-stats-ui.js`'s `murdokuScreen` renders solved, total and per tier. In
  `js/players-agg.js`'s SOLO set (a solve is a run, not a win over anyone).

## Tests

`node murdoku/js/test.js` (in `run-all-tests.mjs`): 40 seeds per tier, each checked for shape,
rules, uniqueness by search, solvable by the human solver at its level, the murderer shape, a
clue for everyone and the difficulty floor; plus determinism, save round trip, `checkBoard`, and
an expert speed budget.
