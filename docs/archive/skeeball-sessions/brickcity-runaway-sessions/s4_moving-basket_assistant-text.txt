## Why it's cheaper than it looks

**There is no hole to move.** `machine.js` builds the tread as one *solid slab* (`skeeball/js/machines/basketball/machine.js:170-180`). A "hole" is not geometry at all — it's a capture rule plus a collision-mask trick: when `physics.js` decides the ball is in, it sets `ball.collisionFilterMask = GROUP_REST` and the ball falls through the whole slab. So the only physical thing at a basket is its **14 collar boxes** (`machine.js:226-252`). Moving a basket = moving 14 boxes and a mesh. Nothing is cut, nothing is re-triangulated.

**Everything downstream is keyed by hole *id*, not position.** `topC` stays `topC` in the autosave, `cupAt()`, the goals, the recorder, the leaderboard. A moving basket is invisible to all of it.

**Your layout choice already solves the hard constraint.** Stripping the top row to just the 100 removes the `holes.spacing` collision (1.30X minimum) that a mover sweeping past two static 50s would violate at every step.

**cannon-es already supports it.** The vendored build has real `KINEMATIC` bodies (`vendor/cannon-es.js:3892`, `:3519`): they integrate by their own velocity, have infinite solve mass so the ball can't shove them, and the contact solver reads their velocity — so a moving rim imparts a proper impulse instead of teleporting through the ball. That last part is why this must be kinematic bodies and *not* "set `body.position` each step" on a static body, which would penetrate and then explode.

**Engines are already per-machine.** Since 2026-08-23 each machine owns its own `physics.js` / `machine.js` / `render.js` (`engines.js`). A mover machine is a copy of `basketball/`, and the changes cannot reach HOT SHOT, BRICK CITY, THE CLASSIC or POPONGO. That's exactly the isolation this needs.

## What actually has to change

**1. Capture must use velocity *relative to the mouth*.** This is mandatory, not polish. `physics.js:302-338` computes `vFace` as the ball's speed across the face and asks "does it fall in before it crosses the mouth?" With a moving mouth, a ball sitting still on the tread reads `vFace ≈ 0` → captured instantly. The basket would hoover up every dead ball it drove over — which is `MACHINE-SPEC.md` section 9's banned magnetism arriving through the back door. Fix is small: subtract the mouth's u-velocity before taking the magnitude.

**2. The committed-capture branch needs the live u.** `physics.js:265-285` re-measures `d` against `hDef.u` while the ball falls through. If the mouth slides on, the ball is re-scored as a `corner0` gutter mid-drop. Cleanest fix is to latch the mouth's u at capture — physically, the ball is in the basket and rides with it.

**3. One rack clock, because two balls can be live at once.** `canThrow()` (`game.js:83-87`) lets ball 2 launch as soon as ball 1 has `arrived`, and **each throw builds its own world** (`physics.js:33`). Two coexisting worlds plus a renderer that builds its own separate `buildMachine` copy (`render.js:64`) means three things have to agree on where the basket is. Driving the phase off per-throw `st.t` would make them disagree and the hoop would visibly snap when ball 2 launches. It needs a rack-level clock on the `Game` instance, passed into `startThrow`, `step` and `render`, with the offset itself computed by one pure exported function (`moverU(G, t)`) that physics and render both import.

That clock is the **one edit to a shared file** (`game.js`), and it's additive — the other four engines ignore the extra argument. Worth naming explicitly in the commit, per `engines.js`'s rule.

**4. Determinism survives, if the phase is passed explicitly.** `test.js:171-172` asserts two identical `simulateThrow` calls match. Keep `t0` an explicit param and that still holds. But the reachability sweep gains a third axis: today it's 41×21 = 861 throws over (power, aim); a mover makes reachability a function of phase too.

**5. The spec tests would lie.** `holes.inside` and `holes.spacing` (`test-skeeball-machine-spec.mjs:107-131`) test `hole.u` as a single number. A mover passes at rest and tells you nothing about its extremes. Those rules need to test the travel envelope, or the board carries a waiver.

## The new failure mode

**The mover can crush a ball against a rail.** `MACHINE-SPEC.md` section 12 already bans a collar within `0.78X` of a flat wall, because a curved wall converging on a flat one makes a pinch that three-contact-locks the solver — measured on POPONGO's first draft at **12% of all throws ending in the watchdog's walkout**. A *moving* collar is strictly worse: it actively drives a ball into that pinch, with infinite solve mass and nothing to yield.

That sets the travel limit, and the number is concrete. Face half-width 0.500 m; the 100's rim is `X * 0.4375` = 0.0636 m radius, plus `collarThick` 0.012 → outer face 0.0756 m; minus the 0.78X (0.1135 m) rail gap:

> **max |u| ≈ 0.311 m — a 0.62 m stroke on a 1.00 m face.**

So it cannot literally reach the left and right edges. But here's the nice part: the existing `topL` and `topR` sit at `u = ±0.301 m`. **The mover's envelope is almost exactly the span the three top hoops already occupy**, and all three are documented clean-capturable with zero emergencies. So reachability across the sweep is already half-proven by the current machine.

Secondary: a dead ball resting on a tread will get *ploughed* by the passing rim. That's arguably charming and true to a real machine, but it's the mechanism that feeds the pinch above.

## How fast should it move

I ran the real basketball engine to get this. On a top-row-capable throw the ball reaches top-row height **0.40–0.50 s after launch**:

| power | t to top row | settled | landed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.72 s | 1.27 s | midC |
| 0.7 | 0.48 s | 1.39 s | **topC** |
| 0.8 | 0.46 s | 1.24 s | **topC** |
| 1.0 | 0.40 s | 2.14 s | midC |

So the player is leading the target by roughly half a second. For that lead to be a real skill and not a coin flip, the basket wants to travel maybe 15–25% of its stroke in that window — about 0.22–0.33 m/s. On a sine (smooth, no velocity discontinuity at the ends, free to compute) that's a **round trip of roughly 6–8 seconds**. A triangle wave would snap-reverse against any ball touching the rim, so I'd start with the sine.
