Skeeball Leaderboard Redesign

The skeeball leaderboard per player looks terrible. And the by game leaderboard is also terrible. Matt, 2026-08-25, from the live board

1What you are designing

Two screens inside the Game Hub's Leaderboard overlay. The hub is a small, ad free, installable PWA hosting about twenty two self contained games. Roughly six real people use it: one family, on phones, mostly through the installed app rather than a browser tab.

Skeeball is one of those games, and it is a solo arcade cabinet. No opponent, no wins, no losses. You throw a rack of nine balls at a machine and you get a score. There are five machines, unlocked in a chain.

ScreenWhere it livesWho sees it
A. The Skeeball board Leaderboard → By Game → Skeeball everyone; it is the public ranking
B. A player's Skeeball screen Leaderboard → tap a player → Skeeball, and the hub's own My Stats → Skeeball everyone (one piece of code renders both)

Screen B is the one Matt called terrible first. Screen A he called terrible second.

2What was already fixed

Do not re design a bug

A data pass landed on 2026-08-25. It corrected what the screens said and deliberately left what they look like alone, which is why the reply to it was "these look identical to before". None of the following needs solving again.

  • Screen B led with Wins / Losses / Tied / Win rate, all four reading zero. Skeeball has had no opponent since 2026-08-11, so those counters are frozen wherever they stopped. They now appear only on a device that actually holds a non zero one, labelled as the earlier vs computer era.
  • Machines rendered as raw translation keys: gs_sk_board_basketball, gs_sk_board_popongo.
  • The per machine table was five columns wide and did not fit a phone, so the whole overlay scrolled sideways. It is a card list now.
  • A "Record by difficulty" table listed the machines as difficulties, each at a meaningless 100%.
  • Screen A ranked a high score game by how many racks each person had thrown, so number one had 502 plays and the scores ran down the list in no order at all (700, 450, 370, 290, 270, 290). It ranks on the best score now.

So the content is right. The layout is still a generic grid of grey tiles and near identical cards. That is the job.

3Screen A: the ranking

Today: a vertical list of near identical rounded cards, one per player. Rank number, emoji avatar, name, one big number on the right with its unit stacked underneath, a chevron, and a small muted second number on a second row. Below the list sits a "WHO LEADS WHAT" grid of pastel tinted chips.

  • Realistic size is six rows. It has never exceeded eight and will not pass about twelve.
  • Every row is a button: tapping it opens that player's screen B. Tap target at least 44px.
  • The viewer's own row is highlighted. Today that is a coloured border, which is not enough on its own (constraint 3).
  • Ranks run 1..N, and today the top three get gold, silver and bronze discs.
  • A Sort by control sits above the list: Alphabetical, Games Played, Points. It defaults to Points here. There is deliberately no difficulty filter on Skeeball, because its buckets are machines rather than difficulties.

daily is a real time series, per machine, per player, going back to the day they started. Nothing in the app draws it. A sparkline, a last fourteen days strip, a personal best trend: all available with no new storage and no new writes.

Goal progress: three objectives per machine

Derived on demand, never stored. Each machine returns three objects of { id, label, now, target, met }. THE CLASSIC wants five 100s ever, 360 in one rack, and 10,000 points on the machine. HOT SHOT wants a ball in all nine baskets, 700 in one rack, and 30,000 points. Completing a machine's three is what unlocks the next machine in the chain. The admin page draws these rails; the player's own screen does not.

Practice racks

Racks thrown while a machine was in testing. They are kept, they are shown on their own quieter row, and they count towards nothing. They must stay visible and must never read as part of the record.

6Constraints

These are not preferences. A design that breaks one of them cannot ship.

  1. 01Phone first, and it has to fit

    Design at 393 × 852, and survive 360 wide and 667 tall. The overlay is full screen and scrolls vertically; the panel is centred at max-width 620px (screen A) and 560px (screen B), under a sticky header of about 44px plus the device safe area. Nothing may ever scroll sideways. That is the exact bug that shipped: wide content scrolls inside its own container or it does not exist.

  2. 02Light and dark, both, by class

    Dark mode is a class on the root element (.gh-dark), never a prefers-color-scheme query, because the player's explicit choice has to win. Every colour needs a value in both. Tokens: --hub-bg #f4f6fb / #0e1420, --hub-surface #ffffff / #1b2333, --hub-surface-2 #eef2f8 / #2b3547, --hub-ink #16243a / #e9eef6, --hub-muted #5b6b82 / #9db0c9, --hub-accent #1769d4 in both. A machine's own palette is a brand colour, not a theme colour: if you use it, make it work on both grounds or tint it.

  3. 03Colourblind safe

    Matt is red/green colourblind. Wherever colour carries meaning it is paired with a shape, an icon, a label or a border, never hue alone. That covers rank medals, the viewer's own row, machine identity, and anything reading as good or bad.

  4. 04#ffce3a is the standing emphasis accent

    App wide, for the currently selected or active thing, and always paired with a non colour indicator.

  5. 05No em dashes in copy

    Commas, colons or parentheses. This is a standing rule for every user facing string in the repo.

  6. 06Text no smaller than 11px, tap targets at least 44px

    The UX floor, and it is enforced by a test suite that fails the build.

  7. 07English and Spanish

    Spanish runs 20 to 30% longer ("Puntos totales", "Mejor lanzamiento", "Partidas anteriores (contra la máquina)"). Every label slot has to survive that without truncating or wrapping into a mess. Machine names never translate.

  8. 08THE LAW: player data is never hidden

    The repo's first rule, written after a migration made a player's history invisible. Working rule 1 is "stored is not enough, data must stay visible": a number no screen shows reads to its owner as deleted. So re rank and re group everything in section 4 freely, but do not design a screen that drops one. Working rule 4 is "never fabricate": no averages, rates or per machine numbers that are not actually stored, and a missing value shows a dash, not a zero.

  9. 09Offline first PWA

    No web fonts, no CDN, no external images, no icon library. System font stack only (system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI", Roboto, sans-serif). Artwork is inline SVG or CSS. Every file is precached by a service worker, so a new asset is real weight on every device.

  10. 10Vanilla CSS, no framework, no build step

    The stylesheet for these screens ships as a string inside a JS module and is injected once. Keep selectors flat and prefixed: lb- for the leaderboard, gs- for the stats screens.

  11. 11Screen B's markup is shared with every other game

    The same gs-* classes render Connect Four's, Yahtzee's and Battleship's stats screens. A Skeeball only look has to be scoped under its own wrapper class, not built by editing the shared primitives.

  12. 12Respect prefers-reduced-motion

    Any animation needs a still fallback that loses no information.

7Why it feels dead

Not a spec, just the read on the current screens:

  • Everything carries the same weight. Nine rounded grey tiles, same size, same colour, a big number and a caption in each. 700 (a personal record, hard won) and 4788 (balls thrown, a number that only ever goes up) are drawn identically.
  • Screen A cannot be scanned. Six identical cards with one number each. You cannot see that the leader has practised eight times harder than anyone else, that two players are tied, or that anybody is improving.
  • The game has a strong physical identity and none of it is on these screens. Skeeball is a wooden cabinet with a lit marquee and a lacquered orange face. The hub's stats chrome is a calm white and blue card grid. The real design question is how much cabinet to bring in: a marquee style header and per machine colour would make these unmistakably Skeeball while the surrounding overlay stays hub.
  • The unused data is where the life is. Per machine daily bests going back weeks, three objectives per machine with live progress, which of the nine baskets you have ever hit, clean racks, points per machine. That is a screen with something to say, instead of a wall of counters.

8What to hand back

Static HTML and CSS, one file per screen, self contained, no JavaScript needed for the look. Ideally:

  • both screens at 393px wide, in light and dark;
  • realistic content: the six players from section 3, the two machines a real player has actually played (THE CLASSIC and HOT SHOT), and six digit numbers where they occur;
  • a thin state as well: a player with five racks on one machine and nothing else;
  • colours taken from the --hub-* tokens rather than hard coded greys, so it drops straight into the theme.

It gets ported into js/leaderboard-ui.js (screen A) and js/game-stats-ui.js (screen B), keeping the class prefixes, then checked at 393 × 852 in both themes with no sideways scroll before it ships.
